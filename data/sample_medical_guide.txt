Section: Blood pressure
For this synthetic guide, an adult blood pressure reading contains systolic and
diastolic values in mmHg. Repeated elevated readings should be reviewed in
clinical context. A single home reading is not enough to establish a diagnosis.
Common documentation fields include date, time, position, cuff/site, systolic,
diastolic, pulse, symptoms, and relevant medication context.

Section: Medication reconciliation
A medication reconciliation note should record the medication name, strength,
route, frequency, indication when known, adherence concerns, allergies, and
whether the list was verified against an available source. Uncertainty should
be explicitly marked instead of silently filling missing information.

Section: Hydration education
For this synthetic example, patient education about hydration should use plain
language, avoid promises, and encourage the person to follow the fluid advice
given by their healthcare professional. Education should mention that needs vary
with activity, environment, age, and medical conditions.

Section: Red-flag escalation
Educational drafts should not diagnose. If a person reports severe or rapidly
worsening symptoms, the software should advise seeking urgent professional
assessment rather than attempting to determine a diagnosis from the text.

Section: Documentation quality
A structured clinical draft should separate reported facts, observations,
assessment statements supplied by the author, and follow-up plans. Missing
information should be labeled "not provided" rather than invented.

Section: Pathology and Biopsy Terminology Reference
- Reactive Lymphoid Follicular Hyperplasia: A benign (non-cancerous) increase in the number of normal immune cells and enlargement of lymphoid follicles within a lymph node. This is a common, healthy physiological response of the immune system to localized infection, inflammation, or vaccination.
- Sinus Histiocytosis: A harmless expansion of histiocytes (macrophages) within lymph node channels, reflecting the lymph node actively filtering and clearing cellular debris from a nearby inflammatory site.
- Gross vs Microscopic Examination: Gross examination refers to visual inspection of the tissue specimen with the naked eye (measuring size, color, texture). Microscopic examination is the detailed analysis of thin tissue slices under a microscope to evaluate cellular architecture.
- Tingible Body Macrophages: Specialized immune clean-up cells found inside active germinal centers; their presence is a classic hallmark of normal, healthy, reactive tissue rather than lymphoma.
- Benign vs Malignant: "Benign" indicates a non-cancerous condition that does not invade other tissues. "Malignant" refers to cancerous growth that can invade or spread.
- Impression / Conclusion: The primary finding or diagnostic conclusion reached by the pathologist after examining the tissue. It serves as the official medical summary of the biopsy.

Section: Laboratory and Blood Test Reference
- Complete Blood Count (CBC): Evaluates overall health and detects a variety of conditions, including infection and anemia, by measuring white blood cells, red blood cells, hemoglobin, and platelets.
- Lipid Profile: Evaluates cardiovascular health by assessing total cholesterol, LDL ("bad" cholesterol), HDL ("good" cholesterol), and triglycerides. Mild elevations are typically managed with lifestyle, diet, and clinical monitoring.
